# Benefit in Kind Calculator UK — P11D Tax on Perks

> A benefit in kind is taxed as if its cash value were added to your salary, at your marginal rate, with no employee National Insurance.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

Medical insurance, gym membership, an interest-free loan over £10,000: each is valued and taxed as income.

| | Benefit in kind | Ordinary salary |
|---|---|---|
| Income Tax | yes, marginal rate | yes |
| Employee NI | **no** | yes |
| Employer NI | Class 1A at 15% | Class 1 |

HMRC usually collects it by **shrinking your tax code** rather than taking it from a payslip line, which is why a benefit can make your take-home fall without any deduction appearing.

Your P11D arrives by 6 July and lists the cash equivalent of everything you received.

## What this does not cover

Company cars have their own rules based on list price and CO₂, not what the employer paid. Some benefits are genuinely tax-free — mobile phones, workplace parking, and up to £500 of pensions advice — and should not be entered.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
